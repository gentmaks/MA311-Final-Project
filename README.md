## Abstract

Caribbean coral reefs have shifted from coral-dominated communities to degraded
reefs covered by macroalgae. Following the mass mortality of the sea urchin
*Diadema antillarum* in 1983–1984, parrotfishes became the main grazers
limiting algal growth. Using the model of Blackwood, Hastings, and Mumby
(2012), we study how fishing and coral-dependent parrotfish carrying capacity
affect reef stability. We compare food limitation, where coral reduces algal
foraging area, with habitat limitation, where coral provides shelter. The
analysis shows that habitat-limited reefs tolerate less fishing and may not
recover naturally after collapse. Simulations further show that moderate
fishing can permit slow recovery under food limitation but cause collapse under
habitat limitation.
